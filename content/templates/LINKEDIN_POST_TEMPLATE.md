# Template — LinkedIn Post
**Governed by:** [`docs/CONTENT_BRAND_GUIDELINES.md`](../../docs/CONTENT_BRAND_GUIDELINES.md)

> LinkedIn voice: professional, lesson-driven, no slang, no hype. Slightly more elevated than IG/TikTok but still plain. 100–250 words. Soft CTA at the end. 0–3 hashtags max.

---

## Structure

**[Line 1 — the hook as a standalone line.]**
*(Make it a complete, scroll-stopping thought. This is the preview line.)*

[Line 2–3 — reframe or stakes. Why this matters / why the common assumption is wrong.]

[Body — 2–4 short paragraphs. One idea. Build it with a concrete scenario from a local business owner's reality. Pain → why it happens (no blame) → what "fixed" looks like, outcome-first.]

[The honest line — where relevant, the proof-standard note: what's real, what's being measured, what we won't fake.]

[Soft CTA — an invitation, not a command. e.g., "If you've never put a number to this, there's a one-minute way to — happy to share."]

#hashtag1 #hashtag2 (optional, ≤3)

## Worked mini-example (from Idea #58, Speed-to-Lead)

> For decades, word of mouth was the growth engine for local businesses. It still matters. But a quieter force increasingly decides who wins the job: speed-to-lead.
>
> Speed-to-lead is just how fast you respond after a customer reaches out. What changed is the expectation around it — customers now live in an instant-response world, and a day-late callback reads as unreliable, not busy.
>
> The businesses responding within seconds — including after hours — are steadily winning work from equally skilled competitors who respond slowly. The work isn't the variable. The response time is.
>
> For any lead-driven business, this is the shift worth getting ahead of — while the advantage is still available.

### LinkedIn do/don't
- ✅ Lessons, frameworks, honest founder reflections, industry trends.
- ✅ White space. Short paragraphs. One idea.
- ❌ Hype words, emojis-as-punctuation, exclamation points, "AI," "chatbot," "software."
- ❌ More than one CTA.
