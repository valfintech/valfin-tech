## 1. The One-Paragraph Brief

Valfin Tech builds **Revenue Operations Infrastructure** for local service businesses — the quiet system that makes sure no inbound call, text, or form ever goes unanswered. We don't help businesses get *more* leads. We make sure they stop losing the ones they already have. Our content exists to make a busy, non-technical business owner feel one specific thing: *"That's me. I'm losing money to missed calls and slow follow-up — and there's a way to fix it."* Every piece of content should be honest, plain-spoken, outcome-first, and free of AI hype. We earn trust by showing real numbers and admitting what we don't know yet — because in a market full of exaggeration, honesty is our advantage.

**The single most important sentence in our positioning:** *"This was never a marketing problem."*

**The line that captures the whole brand:** *"Your competitors aren't better than you. They just call back faster."*

## 2. Who We Are Talking To

We are talking to **non-technical local service business owners** who are busy, overwhelmed, and focused on ROI.

**Primary verticals:** Roofing (flagship), HVAC, Plumbing, Electrical — plus Solar, General Contractors, Real Estate, Insurance, Legal, Dental, Med Spas, Consulting (the 12 industries on the website).

**They must meet all four ICP criteria:**
1. New revenue arrives through inbound calls, texts, forms, or referrals.
2. The owner-operator (or a small team) is the person who feels the pain of a missed call.
3. A missed response visibly costs money — an unanswered call is a lost job.
4. They currently handle intake manually: voicemail, a shared inbox, "whoever picks up," sticky notes.

**What's going on in their head:**
- "I'm on a roof / under a sink / on a job site — I can't answer every call."
- "By the time I call back, they already hired someone else."
- "I know I'm losing work but I don't know how much."
- "I don't have time to learn another app or babysit another dashboard."
- "I've been burned by tech people who overpromised."

**Write for them:**
- ~8th-grade reading level. Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Talk about *their* Tuesday afternoon, not our technology.
- Respect their intelligence and their time. They are experts at their trade, not at software.

## 3. What We Sell (in audience language)

Internally it's the **Revenue Recovery System**. To the audience, describe it by what it *does*, in four plain steps:

| Stage | Say it like this |
|---|---|
| **Capture** | "Every call, text, and form gets caught the second it comes in — even at 9pm on a Sunday." |
| **Respond** | "The customer gets a real, professional reply within seconds — in your business's voice." |
| **Follow up** | "If they don't book right away, it follows up for you — automatically — until they do or clearly say no." |
| **Book** | "The ones who are ready end up as an appointment on your calendar." |

**Three tiers** (never publish prices): **Foundation**, **Growth** (the default recommendation), **Built for You**.

**The ROI tool:** the **Lead Leak Calculator** at `valfintech.com/calculator`. It shows a business owner their *own* number — what they're losing every month to slow response. This is the hero of most conversion content.

**What it is NOT — and content must never imply otherwise:**
- It does **not** generate new leads.
- It is **not** a chatbot on a website.
- It does **not** run with zero human oversight.
- It is **not** "AI software."

## 4. The Positioning Guardrails

These are hard rules pulled directly from the Operating Manual §5. They are not stylistic preferences — they are positioning law.

1. **Never lead with "AI."** AI is one component inside a larger system. The mechanism is always secondary to the outcome. A viewer should understand the *benefit* before they ever hear *how* it works.
2. **We are infrastructure, not software.** We run this for the client; they don't operate it. Never call it an app, a tool, a platform, or a SaaS product.
3. **We recover revenue; we do not generate leads.** Never promise, imply, or hint at "more leads," "more traffic," or "better marketing."
4. **"Chatbot" is the enemy word.** It actively undercuts the positioning. If a piece of content could be mistaken for selling a website chatbot, rewrite it.
5. **The category is Revenue Operations Infrastructure** — but you rarely say that phrase out loud to a business owner. You *demonstrate* it by talking about revenue, response speed, and reliability — not by naming the category.
6. **Pain first, always.** Start with the problem the owner feels. Earn the right to talk about the solution.

## 5. Brand Voice & Tone Rules

**Voice = the personality (constant). Tone = the mood (flexes by context).**

### Voice (never changes)
- **Plain.** Business-owner vocabulary. No startup-speak, no developer-speak.
- **Honest.** We say what's real, including what we don't know yet.
- **Practical.** Every piece leaves the viewer with something true and useful.
- **Calm and confident.** We don't shout. We don't hype. We're the steady operator, not the loud salesperson.
- **Human.** We sound like a person who has actually sat across from a roofer, not like a brand.

### Tone (flexes)
- **Educational content:** patient, clear, generous — like a good mentor.
- **Pain-point content:** empathetic and direct — name the pain plainly, no melodrama.
- **Proof/results content:** understated and factual — let the numbers carry it.
- **Founder content:** personal, reflective, grounded — real story, no bravado.

### The Five-Second Clarity Test
If a business owner can't understand the claim in one read, rewrite it. Every hook, every caption first line, every on-screen headline must pass this test.

### Formatting voice
- Short sentences. Short paragraphs. White space is a feature.
- No exclamation points.
- No emoji spam. At most, sparing, purposeful use on social captions (never in headlines or on-screen video text).
- Specific numbers and concrete scenarios over abstractions.

## 6. The Words We Use & The Words We Never Use

### ✅ Use these (the outcome vocabulary)
Stop losing leads · Call back faster · Respond in seconds · Never miss a call · Book more jobs · Stop the leak · Recover lost revenue · Follow up automatically · After hours, weekends, holidays · Save time · Sound bigger than you are · Run like a larger company · Reliable system · In your voice · Done for you · We run it for you.

### ❌ Never use these (hard banned list — Operating Manual §5)
| Banned | Why |
|---|---|
| "AI Employee" (as our identity) | Frames us as a player in a crowded feature-fight category |
| "Chatbot" / "bot" | Chatbots answer questions on websites; this undercuts everything |
| "Software" / "SaaS" / "app" / "tool" / "platform" | We are infrastructure we run for clients, not software they operate |
| "Results may vary" / any hedging | Erodes the honesty that is our entire advantage |
| Any unverified statistic as proof | Violates the proof standard; one fabrication poisons the brand |
| "More leads" / "lead generation" / "get you customers" | We recover existing leads; this blurs the positioning |
| "Revolutionary" / "game-changing" / "cutting-edge" / "next-gen" | Hype words; we don't sound like this |
| "Unlock" / "supercharge" / "10x" / "skyrocket" | Marketing-bro vocabulary; not our voice |
| Exclamation points | Not our tone |
| Published prices / "$X/month" | We never publish prices; the calculator sets the frame |

> **Rule of thumb:** If a sentence would sound at home in a generic "AI agency" ad, delete it.

## 7. The Proof Standard (Honesty Rules for Claims)

This is a competitive advantage, not just an ethics policy. Follow it exactly (Operating Manual §5).

**Every proof claim must be one of two things:**
1. A specific, verifiable number from a real client's own records, **or**
2. Clearly labeled as "in progress" / "being measured."

**Until the roofing case study is published (60–90 days after client #1 goes live), we have NO published outcome numbers of our own.** This means content must not say or imply things like "our clients book 30% more jobs" or "businesses recover $12,000/month with Valfin." We don't have that proof yet.

**What we CAN do honestly right now:**
- Use **industry-level and common-sense framing**: "Studies and plain experience say most people hire the first business that calls back." (Frame as general truth, not a Valfin result.)
- Use the **Lead Leak Calculator** — it shows the *prospect's own* math, which is honest because it's their inputs.
- Tell the **founding-partner story** truthfully: "We built this inside one real roofing company. It's live right now. We're measuring exactly what it's doing — and we'll show those numbers when we have them."
- Use **hypothetical illustrations clearly labeled as examples**: "Say you get 40 calls a month and miss 10..." (a worked example, not a claimed result).

**When in doubt, the honest move is to say what's true and what isn't yet known.** "We'll tell you when we know, and here's how we're measuring it" is more powerful than any invented number.

## 8. Message Architecture: Hooks, Bodies, CTAs

### Hooks (first 1–2 seconds / first line)
The hook names the pain or the stakes. It must pass the five-second test and stop the scroll for a business owner specifically.

**Hook formulas that fit our brand:**
- **The gut-punch stat (general, honest):** "Most customers hire whoever calls back first. Not whoever's best."
- **The relatable miss:** "You were on a roof. The phone rang. They called the next guy."
- **The reframe:** "You don't have a marketing problem. You have a follow-up problem."
- **The cost question:** "How many jobs did you lose last month — without ever knowing?"
- **The after-hours angle:** "Your best lead this week called at 7:42pm. Did anyone answer?"

### Body (the 15–45 seconds in between)
- One idea. Build it plainly. Use a concrete scenario.
- Pain → why it happens (no blame on the owner) → what "fixed" looks like → light proof/credibility.
- Never more than one teaching point per piece.

### CTAs (calls to action)
Match the CTA to the funnel stage (see Pillars and the 90-Day Strategy). Default CTAs:

| Funnel stage | CTA | Where it points |
|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | "Follow for more on plugging the leaks in your business." | Profile / follow |
| **Consideration** | "See exactly what slow follow-up is costing you — link in bio." | `valfintech.com/calculator` |
| **Conversion** | "Want us to set this up for your business? Send us a message." | `valfintech.com` → "Talk to us" / `/company#contact` |

- **Primary site CTA:** "See My Numbers" → the Calculator.
- **Secondary site CTA:** "Talk to us" → contact.
- Never use more than one CTA per piece of content. One ask.
- CTAs are calm invitations, not commands. No "DM ME NOW," no urgency theater.

## 12. The Pre-Publish Checklist

Before ANY piece of content goes live, it must pass all of these. (Printable version: `content/checklists/CONTENT_APPROVAL_CHECKLIST.md`.)

- [ ] **Outcome-first:** Leads with a pain or outcome the owner feels — not with technology.
- [ ] **No banned words:** Scanned against §6. No "AI Employee," "chatbot," "software," hype words, or exclamation points.
- [ ] **AI is secondary:** If AI is mentioned at all, it comes after the benefit and is framed as one part of a system.
- [ ] **Proof is honest:** Every number is either a verified real client figure or clearly labeled as a hypothetical example / in-progress measurement (§7).
- [ ] **No lead-gen promise:** Nothing implies we generate new leads.
- [ ] **Five-second clarity:** The hook and main claim are understandable in one read.
- [ ] **8th-grade reading level:** Short sentences, plain words.
- [ ] **One CTA:** Exactly one ask, matched to the funnel stage.
- [ ] **Right pillar:** Maps cleanly to one of the five content pillars.
- [ ] **On-brand tone:** Calm, honest, practical, premium-but-approachable. No shouting.

## 13. How AI Should Use This Document

When generating Valfin content, an AI system (Claude or otherwise) must:

1. **Load this document first**, plus the relevant production/template file. Treat it as constraints, not suggestions.
2. **Treat §6 (banned words) and §7 (proof standard) as hard filters.** Self-check every draft against them before returning it. If a draft contains a banned word or an unverifiable claim, regenerate — do not return it.
3. **Default to pain-first, outcome-first structure** for every script and caption.
4. **Never invent statistics or case-study results.** If a number is needed and none is verified, use a clearly-labeled hypothetical example or omit it.
5. **Match the funnel stage to the CTA** per §8.
6. **Preserve the simplification philosophy.** Don't add complexity, jargon, or feature-talk where a plain outcome sentence does the job.
7. **When uncertain, choose the more honest and more plain option.** That is almost always the on-brand choice for Valfin.
