# Proposal Playbook
_Created 2026-06-10 — how to fill out and send `CLIENT_PROPOSAL_TEMPLATE.docx`, and how it bridges from the discovery call into the agreement, payment, onboarding, and kickoff. Read alongside `CLIENT_ACCEPTANCE_FLOW.md` (the full proposal→paying-client flow) and `DISCOVERY_CALL_WORKBOOK.md` (where the inputs for this come from)._

---

## When to send the proposal

**Within 24-48 hours of the discovery call** — ideally same day or next morning, while the conversation is still fresh for the prospect. Per `DISCOVERY_CALL_WORKBOOK.md` Step 4, this applies whether the outcome was "yes, send something" (A) or "let me think about it" (B) — in both cases, the proposal itself does ongoing selling work even when you're not in the room.

---

## What you need before you start

Pull these from the completed `DISCOVERY_CALL_NOTES_TEMPLATE.md` for this prospect:

- Business name and contact name
- The recommended tier (default: **Growth** — see `PRICING_PACKAGING.md`; only propose Foundation if the discovery call revealed a smaller lead volume or budget that makes Growth a mismatch, per Step 4 objection-handling guidance in the workbook)
- The Lead Leak Calculator result from the call (their specific monthly-loss estimate)
- Any specific pain points or language they used — these go into the "Your Situation" section to make the proposal feel personal, not templated
- The setup fee and monthly fee for the chosen tier — **pull the actual dollar figures from `PRICING_PACKAGING.md`'s internal anchors**. Remember: those internal numbers are for *your* use in proposals and conversations — they are never published on the website, but they ARE what goes into a specific client's proposal once you're quoting that client directly.

## How to fill out `CLIENT_PROPOSAL_TEMPLATE.docx`

The template has the following sections — fill in every `[BRACKETED]` field:

1. **Cover page** — client business name, date, prepared-by (Valfin Tech)
2. **Your Situation** — 2-3 sentences reflecting back what they told you on the call (their current process, the specific pain point they mentioned). This section is what makes the proposal feel like it was written *for them* — never leave this generic.
3. **The Opportunity** — restate their Lead Leak Calculator result from the call: "Based on our conversation, we estimate [Business Name] may be losing approximately $[X]/month to slow lead follow-up." This is the same number from Step 2 of the discovery call — repetition reinforces it.
4. **The Solution** — a plain-language description of what's included, drawn from the chosen tier's workflow list in `PRICING_PACKAGING.md` (e.g., for Growth: instant lead capture and response, missed-call text-back, lead scoring, automated follow-up sequences, appointment booking, monthly ROI reporting). Use outcome language ("you'll know within seconds when a hot lead comes in"), not workflow numbers — workflow IDs are internal implementation detail, not client-facing language.
5. **Investment** — the two numbers:
   - One-time setup fee: $[X]
   - Monthly platform fee: $[Y]
   - A single sentence on what the setup fee covers (configuration + carrier verification) and that the monthly fee is month-to-month with no long-term lock-in (per `CLIENT_SERVICE_AGREEMENT_TEMPLATE.md` §3)
6. **The Founding Partner Note** — a short paragraph using the founding-partner framing from `CLIENT_ACQUISITION_PLAYBOOK.md` (also taught in Founder Training Module 2): early-partner attention, input into the system's evolution, founding pricing, in exchange for permission to use their (anonymized or named, their choice) results in case studies later.
7. **What Happens Next** — a short numbered list mirroring `CLIENT_ACCEPTANCE_FLOW.md` Steps 3-9 in plain language: sign agreement → setup payment → onboarding questionnaire → configuration (~timeframe) → go-live walkthrough. This is what makes the proposal feel like the start of a guided process, not a sales document waiting for a counter-offer.
8. **Validity** — a simple note that the pricing above is valid for [30 days] from the proposal date (protects against stale internal-pricing drift if `PRICING_PACKAGING.md` anchors change later)

## Sending the proposal

**Email/message template:**

> Subject: [Business Name] — Proposal
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Great talking with you yesterday — really enjoyed learning more about how [Business Name] runs day to day.
>
> Attached is a short proposal based on what we discussed, including the numbers specific to your business. Take a look whenever's convenient — happy to answer any questions, or hop on a quick call if that's easier.
>
> If everything looks good, the next step is simple: a short service agreement, and then we can get started right away.
>
> Talk soon,
> [Founder name]

Attach `CLIENT_PROPOSAL_TEMPLATE.docx` (filled in and ideally exported/saved as a PDF for a more polished, non-editable feel — "Save As PDF" from Word/Google Docs).

## After sending

- Log the send date in the prospect tracker (`CLIENT_ACQUISITION_PLAYBOOK.md`)
- If outcome was (B) "thinking it over" from the discovery call, this proposal **is** the follow-up material — set your 2-week check-in date as planned
- When the client responds positively → proceed to `CLIENT_ACCEPTANCE_FLOW.md` Step 3 (service agreement)

## After your first 3-5 proposals — close the loop

This template and structure are a first draft. After sending a handful of real proposals:

1. **If a client asks "why is the setup fee what it is" or pushes back on the monthly fee**, note exactly what they said — this is signal for whether the "Investment" section needs more justification language (e.g., expanding on what the setup fee covers)
2. **If clients consistently ask the same question that isn't answered in the proposal** (e.g., "what if I want to cancel," "do you offer support"), consider adding a short FAQ section to the template
3. **If the founding-partner framing generates pushback or confusion in writing** (it may land differently in text than verbally), that's worth flagging — the verbal framing was validated in Founder Training Module 2, but written framing may need adjustment
4. Any changes to the template itself should stay consistent with the brand direction in `valfin-brand-foundation` — Navy/Blue palette, Revenue Operations Infrastructure positioning, not "AI Employee" language
