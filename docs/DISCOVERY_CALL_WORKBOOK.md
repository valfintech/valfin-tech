# Discovery Call Workbook
_Created 2026-06-10 — the live, run-the-call companion to `CLIENT_ACQUISITION_PLAYBOOK.md`. That playbook tells you how to get the call booked; this document is what you open during the call itself._

> **What this is:** a structured 15-minute call script with the exact words, in the exact order, that turns "sure, fifteen minutes" into "yes, let's do this" — or, just as usefully, into a well-tracked "not yet."
>
> **What this is not:** a rigid script to read verbatim. The four-step structure (listen → calculate → offer → handle the answer) is the skill — Module 3 of `FOUNDER_TRAINING_PLAN.md` covers why this exact sequence matters. Use this workbook to stay on structure while sounding like yourself.

## The 15-minute structure

### Step 1 — Open by listening (3–4 minutes)

**Goal:** earn trust, surface real pain, and gather the raw numbers you'll need for Step 2 — without pitching anything yet.

**Opening line (adapt to however the call was booked):**
> "Thanks for making time — before I show you anything, I'd actually love to just understand how things work for you today. Can you walk me through what happens when someone calls or messages your business with a job — especially if it's after hours or you're on a roof/in a truck/with another customer?"

**If you did a mystery-customer test on this business:**
> "Actually — I'll be upfront — I called your office last week as a 'customer' to see what happens. [Describe what happened, plainly, no judgment in your tone.] Is that pretty typical, or was that an off day?"

This single sentence does more than any pitch. Most owners will laugh, sigh, or say "yeah, that's about right" — and from there the conversation runs itself.

**Listen for, and write down (Notes Template Section 2):**
- How they currently handle inbound calls/leads (who answers, what happens after-hours, weekends)
- Roughly how many leads/calls they get per month (you'll need this for Step 2)
- Roughly what an average job is worth to them (you'll need this for Step 2)
- Any specific frustration they mention unprompted — write it down **verbatim if possible**. This is gold for the Scorecard.

**Don't do yet:** explain what Valfin is, mention pricing, or describe the system. If they ask "so what's this about?" early, give the 10-second version from Module 2 and steer back: *"I'll show you exactly what that looks like in a second — first I want to make sure I understand your situation so the numbers I show you are actually yours, not generic."*

---

### Step 2 — Run the Lead Leak Calculator with their numbers (3–4 minutes)

**Goal:** let a number *they generated* do the persuading. Don't narrate over it — let the silence after the number lands do the work.

**Transition line:**
> "Okay — so based on what you just told me, let me show you something. There's a calculator on our site that takes your actual numbers and shows what slow follow-up is probably costing you every month. Let's plug in your numbers right now."

1. Open `https://valfintech.com/calculator`
2. Enter their **monthly leads/calls** number (from Step 1)
3. Enter their **average job value** (from Step 1)
4. Let the result render. **Pause. Let them react first.**

**If they react ("wow" / "that can't be right" / "huh"):**
> "I know — and to be clear, this is a conservative estimate. It assumes you're only losing about 30% of leads to slow response, and only about a third of *those* would've actually converted. For a lot of businesses it's worse than that, not better."

**If they push back on the assumptions:**
> "Totally fair to question it — that's the point of using your real numbers instead of a made-up example. Even if the real number is half of this, is that something worth fixing?"

**Write down (Notes Template Section 3):** the inputs they gave you and the resulting number. You'll want this for the proposal.

---

### Step 3 — Make the founding-partner offer (3–4 minutes)

**Goal:** transition from "here's the problem" to "here's what fixing it looks like" — and make the no-case-study-yet positioning the most memorable thing said in the call.

**Transition line:**
> "So here's what this actually is. [Give the 2-minute version from Module 2 — the missed-call story, the form-capture-and-scoring story, the follow-up story, tailored to what they told you in Step 1.]"

**Then, the founding-partner offer — verbatim from `CLIENT_ACQUISITION_PLAYBOOK.md`:**
> "I'll be straight with you — we're early. We built this system inside one real roofing company here in the Boston area, it's live and running their business right now, and we're in the middle of measuring exactly what it's done for them so we can publish real, verified numbers — not estimates, the actual before-and-after from their own records. You'd be one of the first businesses outside that one to run it. Here's what that means for you: you get more attention during setup than a client #50 ever will, you get a say in how it evolves, and you lock in founding pricing that won't be on the table once we have a track record to charge a premium against. In exchange, I'm going to ask if we can use your real numbers — good or not-as-good — in how we tell this story afterward, the same honest way I'm telling it now."

**On pricing — never state a number first:**
> "On cost — it depends on your situation, which is part of why I wanted to understand how things work for you first. What I can tell you is the structure: there's a one-time setup, because this is real infrastructure being installed for your business specifically, and then a flat monthly fee after that — month to month, no long-term lock-in. I'll put together exact numbers for your situation and send them over as a short proposal — that work for you?"

If they push for a ballpark, you may give your **Growth**-tier internal anchor range (see `PRICING_PACKAGING.md`) — but frame it as a range, not a quote: *"For a business your size, most engagements land somewhere in the [$X–$Y setup / $A–$B per month] range — but I want to scope it properly before I commit to a number."*

---

### Step 4 — Handle whatever they say next (2–3 minutes)

This is the step most people skip planning for — and it's the one that determines whether a "not now" becomes a future yes or a dead lead.

#### A) "Yes, let's do it" / "Send me something"
> "Great — I'll put together a short proposal with exactly what's included and the numbers for your situation, and send it over by [tomorrow / specific day]. Once you're good with it, the next step is a simple service agreement and we get started — most businesses are live within a few weeks."

→ Move to `PROPOSAL_PLAYBOOK.md` immediately after the call. Set a calendar reminder for the proposal send date.

#### B) "Let me think about it" / "I need to talk to [partner/spouse/etc.]"
> "Of course — totally reasonable. Can I send over that proposal anyway, so you've got the actual numbers in front of you while you think it over? And if it's helpful, I'm happy to hop on a quick call with [the other person] too, if that makes the decision easier."

→ Send the proposal regardless (it does the selling while you're not in the room). Set a follow-up date **2 weeks out**, write it in the tracker.

#### C) "Not right now" / timing objection
> "No problem at all — can I check back in with you in [a month / after your busy season / whatever they say]? I'll keep an eye out and might have a real before-and-after number from our first client by then too, which might make this an easier yes."

→ Log in tracker with the specific follow-up date they gave you. **This is the most common outcome of any first sales call, ever — it's not a loss.**

#### D) Price objection ("that's more than I expected")
> "I hear you — and to be clear, that number isn't arbitrary. [Reference the calculator number from Step 2]. If this recovers even one job a month you'd otherwise lose, it's already paid for itself. But let's back up — what were you expecting, roughly? I want to make sure I'm proposing the right scope for you, not just a fixed package."

→ This often reveals they're thinking "Foundation" not "Growth," or that they have a smaller lead volume than assumed. Adjust the proposal scope accordingly — don't discount the price, change the scope.

#### E) Skepticism ("does this actually work" / "sounds too automated")
> "Totally fair question — and honestly, that's exactly why I led with the fact that we don't have a polished case study yet. I'd rather show you the real thing than convince you with a slide. Want me to show you an actual text message a real customer received from this system?"

→ If you have access to the live CRM/SMS examples (Module 4 demo material), this is the moment to pull them up.

## Common objections — quick reference

| Objection | Response approach |
|---|---|
| "We already have someone answering phones" | "That's great for business hours — this is mainly for the gaps: after-hours, weekends, when your team's on a job. It's not a replacement, it's a backstop." |
| "We tried [other automation tool] before and it felt robotic" | "That's a really common experience — most of those tools send the same message to everyone. This is built and worded specifically for your business, in your voice, and I'll show you exactly what it'll say before it ever goes live." |
| "I don't have time to set this up" | "That's actually the point — the setup is on us. Your part is about [X minutes] of answering questions about how your business runs. We do the configuration." |
| "What if it sends something wrong / embarrassing to a customer?" | "Every message is reviewed and approved by you before go-live, and the system has built-in safeguards — like automatically honoring 'STOP' replies. Nothing goes out that you haven't seen first." |
| "Can I just try it for a month first?" | "I get the instinct — but the setup fee reflects real one-time work (carrier verification, configuration, testing) that doesn't scale down for a trial. What I can offer instead is month-to-month after that, so if it's not working for you, you're not locked in long-term." |

## After the call

1. Fill in `DISCOVERY_CALL_NOTES_TEMPLATE.md` completely while it's fresh — within the hour, not "later"
2. Fill in `DISCOVERY_CALL_SCORECARD.md` for this call
3. Update the prospect tracker (`CLIENT_ACQUISITION_PLAYBOOK.md` format) with outcome + next action date
4. If outcome was (A) or (B) above: open `PROPOSAL_PLAYBOOK.md` and start the proposal **same day** — momentum matters here exactly as much as it does at intake (`ONBOARDING_SOP.md` Phase 1)

## After your first 3–5 calls — close the loop

This workbook is a first draft. After 3–5 real calls:
1. Review your Scorecards together — look for **patterns**, not one-off feedback (see `DISCOVERY_CALL_SCORECARD.md` for what to look for)
2. If the same objection comes up 2+ times, add it to the "Common objections" table above with whatever response actually worked
3. If the calculator's assumptions (30% lost-lead rate, 35% recoverable conversion) consistently feel "too high" or "too low" to prospects, note that — it may be worth flagging to whoever maintains `website/src/lib/calculator.ts`, but **do not change those numbers yourself without discussing** — they're a deliberate, documented assumption, not a styling detail
4. If your **Growth**-tier anchor price consistently lands as "way more than expected" or "barely a pause," that's signal for `PRICING_PACKAGING.md` — but again, treat 1 data point as noise and 3+ as signal
